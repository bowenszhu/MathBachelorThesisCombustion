\chapter{Chemical reaction network with Julia} \label{ch:network}

This chapter presents solving reaction networks with Julia. It starts from the formal problem definition. Examples of 5-node 5-edge networks are illustrated. At the end, the performance of solving large networks with various solver algorithms are evaluated and compared. 

\section{Mathematical model}

As briefly introduced in section~\ref{sec:CRN}, an internal combustor space is modelled as a network of graph $G$ with $n$ vertices $V$ and $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ edges $E$. A vertex $v$ is a container with chemical reactions perfectly stirred which only involve chemistry. An edge $e$ is a pipe serving as a channel for flowing species with prescribed velocity which captures transport.

Each vertex $v_i$ contains $s$ chemical species. Edge $e_{i,j}$ connects vertex $v_i$ and vertex $v_j$. $\mathbf u(t)$ is a vector of unknown species concentration.

\begin{align}
    \mathbf u(t) &= 
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \mathbf u_1(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf u_i(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf u_n(t)
    \end{pmatrix} \\
    \mathbf u_i(t) &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        u_{i,1}(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        u_{i,j}(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        u_{i,s}(t)
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf u_i(t)$ is a subvector corresponding to vertex $v_i$ and $u_{i,j}$ denotes the concentration of the $j$-th species in vertex $v_i$.

The dynamics of the entire system is modelled by a set of ordinary differential equations (ODEs)
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm d \mathbf u (t)}{\mathrm d t} = \mathbf F ( \mathbf u (t), t) =  \mathbf F_{\mathrm{chemistry}} ( \mathbf u (t), t) + \mathbf F_{\mathrm{transport}} ( \mathbf u (t), t)
\end{equation}
with prescribed initial conditions
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf u(t = 0) = \mathbf u_0.
\end{equation}

The aim is to solve the large-scale ODE system with sufficient accuracy for a certain time range. The differential equations are stiff as it involves fast chemistry and slow transport, for which we will apply implicit time integration methods with variable time steps.

\section{5-node 5-edge networks}

In this section, we apply Julia to solve networks consisting of 5 nodes and 5 edges in two different connectivity configurations. Each vertex in the graph is a node with the one-step reaction mechanism~\eqref{re:one-step} of methane combustion as its internal chemistry. The ODE system is defined as follows, $\forall i \in \{1,2,3,4,5\}$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm d \mathbf u_i(t)}{\mathrm d t} = \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        u_{i,1} \\
        u_{i,2} \\
        u_{i,3} \\
        u_{i,4}
    \end{pmatrix}
    % = \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t}
    % \begin{pmatrix}
    %     [\ce{CH4}]_i \\
    %     [\ce{O2}]_i \\
    %     [\ce{CO2}]_i \\
    %     [\ce{H2O}]_i
    % \end{pmatrix}
    % = k [\ce{CH4}]_i^{n_{\ce{CH4}}} [\ce{O2}]_i^{n_{\ce{O2}}}
    % \begin{pmatrix}
    %     -1 \\
    %     -2 \\
    %     1 \\
    %     2
    % \end{pmatrix}
    = k u_{i,1}^{n_{\ce{CH4}}} u_{i,2}^{n_{\ce{O2}}}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        -1 \\
        -2 \\
        1 \\
        2
    \end{pmatrix}
    + \sigma \sum_{j=1}^5 G_{ji} (\mathbf u_j - \mathbf u_i)
\end{equation}
where $u_{i,1}$, $u_{i,2}$, $u_{i,3}$, $u_{i,4}$ denote the concentration of \ce{CH4}, \ce{O2}, \ce{CO2} and \ce{H2O} in the $i$-th vertex respectively. The rate coefficient $k = A T^\beta \exp(- E_a / R T)$, the reaction orders $n_{\ce{CH4}}$ and $n_{\ce{O2}}$ are the same with the values in table~\ref{tab:one-step} on page~\pageref{tab:one-step}. The graph structure and the flow rate are captured by the parameters $\sigma$ and $G_{ji}$.

\begin{equation}
    G = \bordermatrix{
        ~ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \cr
        1 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \cr
        2 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \cr
        3 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \cr
        4 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \cr
        5 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \cr}
\end{equation}
where the numbers outside the border of the matrix indicate the indices of vertices. The element $G_{j,i}$ on the $j$-th row and the $i$-th column is 1 if there exists a directed edge from vertex $j$ to vertex $i$ and 0 otherwise.
