\chapter{Chemical reaction network with Julia} \label{ch:network}

This chapter presents solving reaction networks with Julia. It starts from the formal problem definition. Examples of the application on 5-node 5-edge networks are illustrated. At the end, the performance of solving large networks with various solver algorithms are evaluated and compared. 

\section{Mathematical model}

As briefly introduced in section~\ref{sec:CRN}, an internal combustor space is modelled as a network of graph $G$ with $n$ vertices $V$ and $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ edges $E$. A vertex $v$ is a container with chemical reactions perfectly stirred which only involve chemistry. An edge $e$ is a pipe serving as a channel for flowing species with prescribed velocity which captures transport.

Each vertex $v_i$ contains $s$ chemical species. Edge $e_{i,j}$ connects vertex $v_i$ and vertex $v_j$. $\mathbf u(t)$ is a vector of unknown species concentration.

\begin{align}
    \mathbf u(t) &= 
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \mathbf u_1(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf u_i(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf u_n(t)
    \end{pmatrix} \\
    \mathbf u_i(t) &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        u_{i,1}(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        u_{i,j}(t) \\
        \vdots \\
        u_{i,s}(t)
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf u_i(t)$ is a subvector corresponding to vertex $v_i$ and $u_{i,j}$ denotes the concentration of the $j$-th species in vertex $v_i$.

The dynamics of the entire system is modelled by a set of ordinary differential equations (ODEs)
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm d \mathbf u (t)}{\mathrm d t} = \mathbf F ( \mathbf u (t), t) =  \mathbf F_{\mathrm{chemistry}} ( \mathbf u (t), t) + \mathbf F_{\mathrm{transport}} ( \mathbf u (t), t)
\end{equation}
with prescribed initial conditions
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf u(t = 0) = \mathbf u_0.
\end{equation}

The aim is to solve the large-scale ODE system with sufficient accuracy for a certain time range. The differential equations are stiff as it involves fast chemistry and slow transport, for which we will apply implicit time integration methods with variable time steps.
